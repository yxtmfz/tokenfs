# Burst recurrence planning: frozen small study

Report 31 fixed-hotset Full: 170.820 MiB/s versus shared SIEVE 187.728; 37 versus 30 copies. Full evicted established hot objects 6,4,1,3,0,7,6 despite the 16 MiB hot set fitting the 24 MiB total cache. The former survival half-life averages consecutive within-run distances together with between-run distances. Four-read bursts can therefore make an established recurrent resident appear overdue long before its next run.

Candidate `burst_interval_survival` (default false) estimates the survival half-life from completed gaps greater than one observed request. It uses only the bounded causal window and requires observed recurrence; one-off bursts retain the former decay, stale keys are still forgotten. Both incoming and resident values use the same estimate. No workload label, fixed hot-key identity, future request or RAM-retention change is used. This is a hypothesis to test, not a guarantee of speedup or a new stand-alone paper contribution.

Twelve runs frozen before execution: Full (candidate), OldFull (report31 policy), PoolSIEVE1; stationary seeds 3271,3291,3292 and moving seed3293. Per run: 576 requests, 2 MiB objects, 24 MiB total disk cache, 4 MiB bounded in-flight capture, identical direct-I/O and durable metadata. Logical partitions remain 8/8/8 for measured policies and 24/0/0 for pooled SIEVE. Only one flag differs between Full and OldFull. Arm order rotates; no run replacement, result-dependent seeds or large sweep.

Primary evidence: bytes/wall-time MiB/s and drained MiB/s; guardrails: p99, correctness and capacity. Positive throughput, p99 nonincrease, drained nondecrease, active changed decisions are the existing v2 screen. Report every pair even when it fails. These short development runs are not confidence intervals, actual GPU measurements, or proof of all paper claims. Distinguish fewer hot evictions/copies from measured planning time: the former can improve total speed without a cheaper individual planner call.
